:::trap
Your margin note on MC 10 says "Cold = sat/short, Heat = unsat/long." That's backwards, and it's probably what cost you the mark. Cold makes membranes stiff, so cells add kinks (unsaturated) and shorter tails to stay fluid. Heat makes membranes too runny, so cells go saturated and longer. Think butter: saturated fat is solid in the fridge.
:::

### Her six question styles

1. "It belongs to both boxes." The sphingomyelin question. Something sits in two categories at once, and the question is built so that picking one box loses the mark. Midterm 2 has plenty of these (list below).
2. One telltale word. "Sialic acid" means ganglioside. For this unit: "GPI-anchored" means ephrinA, "Gly 12" means RasD, "Tyr 527" means c-Src, "470 nm" means ChR2, "his- Salmonella" means the Ames test, "TTAGGG" means telomeres, "hydroxylated" means HIF being destroyed, "chromosome 13" means Rb, "18q" or "DPC" means Smad4, "FAP" means APC, "R206H" means FOP, "CpG island" means methylation silencing.
3. Which direction? Temperature and fatty acids last time. This time: cAMP up or down, cGMP up or down, depolarize or hyperpolarize, anterior or posterior, ion in or ion out.
4. Two blanks in order. "Target ___ for X, ___ for Y." Write each answer next to its blank before you look at the options.
5. Break one part, predict the result. You aced these. Expect cholera toxin, RasD, PDE inhibitors, dominant negatives, VHL loss, Gleevec.
6. Analogies. The cocktail party / talking to yourself / radio question. Expect the same playful framing (light switch, gas pedal, alarm clock).

She also likes "Which is NOT" and "Which is INCORRECT" stems. Circle the NOT before reading the options.

### The two-mark short answer formula

She marks short answers as verdict + reason, one point each. The reason has to name the specific molecular feature.

Verdict (yes/no, true/false, increase/decrease) + the exact feature that proves it + one-line consequence.

Example done right: "The student is incorrect. Sphingomyelin has a phosphocholine head group, so it is a phospholipid as well as a sphingolipid, and it belongs in both boxes."

Example for this unit: "True, the cAMP stays high. Cholera toxin ADP-ribosylates Gαs so it can't hydrolyze GTP, which leaves adenylyl cyclase on."

:::hook
Verdict, Feature, Fallout. "VFF": say the answer, name the molecule part that proves it, say what happens next.
:::

### Sphingomyelin-style traps for midterm 2 (things that belong to two boxes)

:::easy
Each of these is a likely "both" question. If an option or a student in the question picks only one box, that's the trap.

- Ras is a small GTPase AND the product of a proto-oncogene. It is a G protein, but a monomeric one, not heterotrimeric.
- Channelrhodopsin-2 is a 7-transmembrane rhodopsin-family protein AND an ion channel. It is not a GPCR.
- OptoXR is an optogenetic tool AND a GPCR.
- Cytokine receptors are enzyme-coupled receptors with no kinase of their own; the kinase (JAK) is a separate protein stuck to them.
- Src, Abl and JAK are tyrosine kinases but NOT receptors (non-receptor tyrosine kinases).
- Genotoxic carcinogens are carcinogens AND mutagens.
- NF1 is a Ras regulator (a GAP) AND a tumor suppressor. Losing it turns on an oncogene pathway.
- HER2 is a normal receptor AND an oncogene when amplified.
- Acetylcholine is excitatory at skeletal muscle (nicotinic channel) AND slows the heart (muscarinic GPCR).
- PKA phosphorylation activates some enzymes AND inhibits others (phosphorylase kinase on, glycogen synthase off). c-Src is turned OFF by phosphorylation at Tyr 527.
- Endocytosis is an RTK off switch AND, for NGF, a way to carry the signal to the cell body.
- Ca2+ is a second messenger in Lecture 5 AND the trigger for transmitter release and contraction in Lecture 6. Ca2+/calmodulin even activates cAMP PDE, so the Ca2+ branch can turn down the cAMP branch.
- PLC-β is the effector (an enzyme), not a second messenger. IP3 and DAG are the second messengers, and DAG is NOT water-soluble (you got that one on MT1).
- Gβγ is not a passive partner. It carries the signal to the heart K+ channel.
- Ephrins are ligands, but ephrinB can also signal into its own cell (reverse signaling).
- The skier's Epo receptor mutation is gain-of-function, like an oncogene, but it caused extra red cells, not cancer. Same for FOP (ACVR1 R206H): gain-of-function receptor, makes bone, not tumors.
- APC and β-catenin share the Wnt pathway, but APC is a tumor suppressor and β-catenin is an oncogene.
- TGFβ is a tumor suppressor early AND pro-oncogenic late. Its name says "transforming," but its normal job is stopping growth.
- TGFβ receptors are receptor kinases but NOT receptor tyrosine kinases (they're Ser/Thr).
- Hereditary retinoblastoma is inherited like a dominant trait, but Rb is recessive inside each cell.
- Epigenetic silencing is loss of function with a perfectly normal DNA sequence.
- Frizzled has 7 transmembrane passes like a GPCR, but Wnt signals through Dishevelled and β-catenin.
:::

### Swap pairs (the laminin/fibronectin kind)

:::easy
Know which side each one goes on. These are where an order swap or direction flip costs you an easy mark.

- Gαs raises cAMP; Gαi lowers it.
- GEF turns ON; GAP turns OFF.
- Rod rhodopsin: 11-cis to all-trans. ChR2: all-trans to 13-cis.
- Vision: cGMP goes DOWN and channels close. Smell: cAMP goes UP and channels open.
- Afferent carries in to the CNS; efferent carries out.
- MS hits CNS myelin; Guillain-Barré hits PNS myelin.
- Nicotinic = channel in skeletal muscle; muscarinic = GPCR in heart.
- Halorhodopsin pumps Cl- IN; bacteriorhodopsin pumps H+ OUT. Both silence.
- Anterior retina goes to posterior tectum; posterior retina (high EphA) stops in anterior tectum.
- ephrinA is GPI-anchored and binds EphA; ephrinB is transmembrane and binds EphB.
- Raf = MAPKKK, MEK = MAPKK, ERK = MAPK.
- Oncogene = gain-of-function, one copy enough. Tumor suppressor = loss, usually both copies.
- Dominant negative = loss-of-function tool. Constitutively active = gain-of-function tool.
- Gene amplification = too much normal protein. Point mutation or fusion = abnormal hyperactive protein.
- Closed Na+ channel can open; inactivated can't.
- Troponin binds Ca2+; tropomyosin is the blocker that moves.
- Benign stays inside the basal lamina; malignant crosses it.
- -mab = antibody; -nib = kinase inhibitor.
- Type II TGFβ receptor phosphorylates type I (never the reverse).
- R-Smad = Smad2/3 (touched by the receptor); co-Smad = Smad4.
- No Wnt: β-catenin phosphorylated and destroyed. Wnt: β-catenin stable.
- Oncogene mutations cluster at hotspots; tumor suppressor mutations scatter and truncate.
- Activin activates FSH; inhibin inhibits it.
- Wnt TCF (with β-catenin, Groucho) vs c-fos TCF (with SRF).
:::
