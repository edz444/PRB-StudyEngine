## Practice in her style {#practice}

These copy the question styles from midterm 1. Answer in your head (or on paper with the VFF formula) before opening each answer. Short-answer marks assume one point for the verdict and one for the reason.

:::q
1\. A cell expresses a GPCR coupled to Gαs. You add a non-hydrolyzable GTP analog. What happens to cAMP and why? (2 pts)
???
cAMP stays high (verdict). Gαs bound to the analog can't hydrolyze it, so it never returns to the GDP state and keeps adenylyl cyclase on (reason). Same logic as cholera toxin and as your MT1 epinephrine question.
:::

:::q
2\. A student files Ras under "G proteins" and her lab partner says it belongs under "proto-oncogene products." Who is right? (2 pts)
???
Both. Ras is a small monomeric GTPase (a G protein) and its gene is a proto-oncogene that becomes an oncogene (RasD) with a Gly 12 mutation. This is the sphingomyelin pattern.
:::

:::q
3\. Which is NOT a second messenger? A. cAMP B. IP3 C. Ca2+ D. phospholipase C-β E. DAG
???
D. PLC-β is the effector enzyme that makes IP3 and DAG.
:::

:::q
4\. A rod cell is kept in the dark, then exposed to light. Which pair is correct? A. cGMP rises; membrane depolarizes B. cGMP falls; membrane hyperpolarizes C. cAMP rises; membrane depolarizes D. cGMP falls; membrane depolarizes
???
B. Light activates transducin, which activates cGMP PDE. cGMP falls, cGMP-gated channels close, and the rod hyperpolarizes.
:::

:::q
5\. To silence a neuron you could express ___; to make it fire you would express ___. A. ChR2; halorhodopsin B. halorhodopsin; ChR2 C. bacteriorhodopsin; halorhodopsin D. OptoXR; bacteriorhodopsin
???
B. Order trap. Halorhodopsin pumps Cl- in (silences). ChR2 lets cations in (fires). Fill each blank first, then match.
:::

:::q
6\. True or false: halorhodopsin and channelrhodopsin-2 both work by opening an ion channel. Explain. (2 pts)
???
False. ChR2 is a light-gated channel; ions flow down their gradient. Halorhodopsin is a light-driven pump that pushes Cl- into the cell.
:::

:::q
7\. A Drosophila mutant lacks Boss. A second fly lacks Sevenless but expresses RasD. Which fly has an R7 cell? (2 pts)
???
Only the sev + RasD fly. Without Boss there's no ligand for Sev, so the precursor becomes a cone cell. RasD is downstream of Sev, so active Ras bypasses the missing receptor.
:::

:::q
8\. Posterior retinal ganglion cell axons avoid posterior tectum because: A. anterior tectum attracts them B. posterior tectum has high ephrinA that repels high-EphA axons C. posterior axons lack Eph receptors D. ephrinB is GPI-anchored
???
B. She wrote on the slide that it's repulsion from posterior tectum, not attraction to anterior.
:::

:::q
9\. A chemical is negative on the Ames test but causes skin tumors in mice when painted repeatedly after one low dose of a mutagen. Classify it and give the mechanism. (2 pts)
???
Non-genotoxic carcinogen (tumor promoter), like phorbol esters/TPA. It mimics DAG and keeps PKC on, forcing already-mutated cells to keep dividing.
:::

:::q
10\. A student says "every mutagen is a carcinogen." Correct them. (2 pts)
???
Incorrect. Some chemicals mutate bacterial DNA (Ames positive) but are repaired by mammalian DNA repair or can't enter animal cells, so they don't cause cancer. Only genotoxic carcinogens are both.
:::

:::q
11\. Which change converts a proto-oncogene into an oncogene by making too much of a NORMAL protein? A. Gly 12 point mutation in Ras B. Bcr-Abl fusion C. HER2 gene amplification D. deletion of c-Src Tyr 527 E. neu Val-to-Gln change
???
C. Amplification overproduces a normal protein. The others make an abnormal, hyperactive protein.
:::

:::q
12\. Is phosphorylation of c-Src on Tyr 527 activating or inhibiting? (2 pts)
???
Inhibiting. P-Tyr 527 binds Src's own SH2 domain and clamps the kinase shut. Deleting Tyr 527 makes Src constantly active (v-Src).
:::

:::q
13\. A patient's kidney cells lack functional VHL. What happens to HIF and VEGF in normal oxygen, and why? (2 pts)
???
HIF stays high and VEGF stays on. VHL is the E3 ubiquitin ligase subunit that tags hydroxylated HIF for destruction; without it, HIF isn't degraded even with oxygen.
:::

:::q
14\. In heart muscle, which subunit opens the K+ channel after acetylcholine binds the muscarinic receptor, and what happens to heart rate?
???
Gβγ (not Gα-GTP). K+ leaves, the membrane hyperpolarizes, and the heart slows.
:::

:::q
15\. Match the analogy: a gas pedal stuck down, brakes that fail, a fired mechanic.
???
Oncogene (stuck gas pedal), tumor suppressor loss (failed brakes), caretaker/DNA repair gene loss (no mechanic, so every other part breaks faster).
:::

:::q
16\. Gleevec treats CML by: A. removing the Philadelphia chromosome B. blocking the ATP-binding pocket of Bcr-Abl C. activating Ras-GAP D. blocking the Epo receptor E. inhibiting telomerase
???
B. It targets the fusion protein's kinase. The translocation is still there.
:::

:::q
17\. Sarin and curare both cause problems at the neuromuscular junction. How do their effects on ACh signaling differ? (2 pts)
???
Sarin blocks acetylcholinesterase, so ACh builds up and signaling is excessive. Curare blocks the nicotinic receptor, so ACh can't signal and muscles are paralyzed.
:::

:::q
18\. A cell is moved from medium with growth factor to medium without it. Which cell keeps dividing: one with a dominant-negative Ras, or one with RasD? Why?
???
The RasD cell. RasD is stuck in the GTP state (GAP can't act), so it signals without any growth factor. Dominant-negative Ras blocks the pathway, so that cell stops.
:::

:::q
19\. A child develops retinoblastoma in both eyes at age 2. A second child develops a single tumor in one eye at age 6, with no family history. Explain the difference using the two-hit model. (2 pts)
???
The first child inherited one defective Rb copy in every cell, so a single additional hit in any retina cell causes a tumor, and with so many cells this happens several times (both eyes). The second child needed two separate hits in the same cell, which is rare, so one tumor appears later.
:::

:::q
20\. A tumor's Rb gene has a completely normal DNA sequence, but no Rb protein is made. What is the most likely explanation?
???
Epigenetic silencing: the Rb promoter's CpG island is methylated (5-methylcytosine added by DNA methyltransferases), condensing the chromatin and shutting off transcription. It still counts as loss of the tumor suppressor.
:::

:::q
21\. In the absence of Wnt, which statement is TRUE? A. β-catenin binds TCF and activates Myc B. Dishevelled pulls axin away from the destruction complex C. β-catenin is phosphorylated by GSK-3 and CK1 and degraded by the proteasome D. Groucho is displaced from TCF E. APC is inactive
???
C. With no Wnt, the destruction complex (axin, APC, GSK-3, CK1) phosphorylates β-catenin and it gets destroyed. A, B and D describe the Wnt-ON state.
:::

:::q
22\. A student says, "APC and β-catenin are both in the Wnt pathway, so they must both be tumor suppressors." Correct them. (2 pts)
???
Incorrect. APC is a tumor suppressor (its loss leaves β-catenin undestroyed), but β-catenin is an oncogene (gain-of-function mutations that block its phosphorylation keep it active). Both changes lead to high β-catenin and Wnt target genes like Myc turned on.
:::

:::q
23\. Put these in order for TGFβ signaling: Smad4 binding, type I receptor phosphorylation, ligand binding, R-Smad phosphorylation, entry into the nucleus.
???
Ligand binding, type I receptor phosphorylation (by type II), R-Smad (Smad2/3) phosphorylation, Smad4 binding, entry into the nucleus.
:::

:::q
24\. What does phosphorylation of an R-Smad do? (2 pts)
???
It changes the R-Smad's shape and unmasks the NLS in its MH1 domain, so the R-Smad, together with Smad4 and importin, can enter the nucleus and regulate transcription.
:::

:::q
25\. Is TGFβ a tumor suppressor or an oncogene? (2 pts)
???
Both, depending on stage. In normal cells and early tumors it inhibits growth (inducing p15 and other cell cycle inhibitors), acting as a tumor suppressor. In late-stage cancer, cells stop responding to its growth brake and it promotes invasion, EMT, immune suppression and angiogenesis.
:::

:::q
26\. Mutation data from 500 tumors show that gene X mutations almost all change the same two amino acids, while gene Y has nonsense mutations scattered along its length. Classify each gene.
???
Gene X is an oncogene (hotspot missense mutations that activate it). Gene Y is a tumor suppressor (truncating mutations anywhere inactivate it).
:::

:::q
27\. A patient's ACVR1 receptor carries R206H. Is this gain- or loss-of-function, and what happens?
???
Gain-of-function. The BMP type I receptor signals even without BMP ligand ("leaky" signaling), causing heterotopic bone formation in muscle and connective tissue (FOP).
:::

:::q
28\. Cells lacking Smad4 are treated with TGFβ. Predict what happens to division and to the extracellular matrix. (2 pts)
???
They keep dividing, because without Smad4 they can't induce p15 and other cell cycle inhibitors. And with less PAI-1 made, proteases break down more extracellular matrix, which favors invasion and metastasis.
:::
